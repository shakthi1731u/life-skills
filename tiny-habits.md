**Tiny Habits**  
**1. Tiny Habits - BJ Fogg**  
**Question 1**  
The idea I found interesting is starting with something very small, like flossing just one tooth or doing one push-up. It shows that starting and being consistent is more important than trying to do too much at once.  

**2. Tiny Habits by BJ Fogg - Core Message**  
**Question 2**  
B = MAP means **Behavior = Motivation, Ability, and Prompt**. A habit becomes easier when I want to do it, when it is simple enough to do, and when I have a reminder or cue to start. Instead of depending only on motivation, I can make the habit easy and connect it to something I already do.  
- **M – Motivation:** How much I want to do the habit.  
- **A – Ability:** How easy or difficult it is to do the habit.  
- **P – Prompt:** A reminder or cue that tells me to do it.
  
**Question 3**  
Celebrating after completing a habit helps me feel good about doing it. That positive feeling, called “Shine,” helps my brain connect the habit with success, making me more likely to repeat it. Even a small celebration like saying “Nice, I did it” can help.  
**3. 1% Better Every Day**  

**Question 4**  
The idea that small improvements compound over time was the most interesting to me. Improving by just 1% each day may not seem like much, but over time those small efforts can lead to a big difference. It reminded me that I don't need to make huge changes all at once; I just need to keep improving consistently.  

**4. Book Summary of Atomic Habits**  
**Question 5**  
The book explains that instead of focusing only on what I want to achieve, I should focus on the kind of person I want to become. For example, instead of only trying to finish a course, I should see myself as someone who learns regularly. My small actions help build that identity over time.  
**Question 6**  
To make a habit easier, I should reduce the effort needed to do it. I can prepare things in advance, remove distractions, and start with a very small step. The easier the habit is to begin, the more likely I am to do it consistently.  
**Question 7**  
To make a habit harder, I can add extra steps between myself and the habit. For example, I can keep distractions away, log out of apps, or put my phone in another room. Making the unwanted habit less convenient reduces the chance of doing it automatically.  

**5. Reflection**  
**Question 8**  
**Habit I want to do more: Studying and learning regularly**  
- **Make the cue obvious:** Keep my laptop and study materials ready on my desk.  
- **Make it attractive:** Remind myself why learning these skills matters for my career.  
- **Make it easy:** Start with just 10 minutes of learning or one small topic.  
- **Make it satisfying:** Mark the task as completed and appreciate that I made time to learn.  
**Question 9**  
**Habit I want to reduce: Spending too much time scrolling on my phone**  
- **Make the cue invisible:** Keep my phone away from my desk and turn off unnecessary notifications.  
- **Make it unattractive:** Remind myself that scrolling for too long takes time away from things I actually want to do.  
- **Make it difficult:** Log out of distracting apps or keep my phone in another room while studying.  
- **Make it unsatisfying:** Track the time I spend scrolling so I can see when it is taking more time than I intended.
